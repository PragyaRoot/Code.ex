01 Core Rules

Rule 1 Basic Principle

Active voice focuses on the doer of the action. Passive voice focuses on the receiver of the action. The SEE examination tests both directions: converting an active sentence into passive voice and converting a passive sentence into active voice.

Active to Passive Formula: Object + correct form of 'be' + Past Participle (V3) + by + Subject Passive to Active Formula: Subject (from the 'by' phrase) + Verb (matching the original tense) + Object (the original passive subject)

Examples of Active to Passive:

  • Present Simple: The chef prepares delicious meals. / Delicious meals are prepared by the chef.
  • Past Simple: The dog bit the postman. / The postman was bitten by the dog.
  • Future Simple: The government will announce the results. / The results will be announced by the government.

Examples of Passive to Active:

  • Present Simple: The gates are checked by the guards every hour. / The guards check the gates every hour.
  • Past Simple: America was discovered by Columbus. / Columbus discovered America.
  • Future Simple: The candidates will be interviewed by the manager. / The manager will interview the candidates.
Rule 3 Pronoun Changes

When the subject moves to the end of the sentence it changes to the objective case. When converting from passive to active the object pronoun changes back to the subjective case.

Rule 4 Special Cases and Advanced Structures

1. Imperatives (Commands)

Active Structure: Verb + Object Passive Structure: Let + Object + be + V3
  • Active to Passive: Submit the form before the deadline. / Let the form be submitted before the deadline.
  • Active to Passive: Wash the dishes immediately. / Let the dishes be washed immediately.
  • Passive to Active: Let the whiteboard be cleaned. / Clean the whiteboard.

2. Imperatives (Negative)

Active Structure: Do not + Verb + Object Passive Structure: Let + Object + not + be + V3
  • Active to Passive: Do not touch the exhibits. / Let the exhibits not be touched.
  • Active to Passive: Do not waste natural resources. / Let natural resources not be wasted.
  • Passive to Active: Let the sealed boxes not be opened. / Do not open the sealed boxes.

3. Interrogatives (Yes/No Questions)

Active Structure: Do/Does/Did + Subject + V1 + Object Passive Structure: Is/Are/Was/Were + Object + V3 + by + Subject
  • Active to Passive: Did the manager sign the contract? / Was the contract signed by the manager?
  • Active to Passive: Does the chef cook the meals? / Are the meals cooked by the chef?
  • Passive to Active: Has the assignment been finished by them? / Have they finished the assignment?

4. Interrogatives (Wh- Questions)

Active Structure: Wh-word + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb + Object Passive Structure: Wh-word + Auxiliary + Object + V3 + by + Subject
  • Active to Passive: Who discovered penicillin? / By whom was penicillin discovered?
  • Active to Passive: Why did they cancel the match? / Why was the match cancelled by them?
  • Passive to Active: Where were the keys found by the guards? / Where did the guards find the keys?

5. Indefinite Subjects (Somebody/Nobody)

Active Structure: Somebody/Nobody + Verb + Object Passive Structure: Object + be + V3 (agent omitted)
  • Active to Passive: Somebody stole the documents. / The documents were stolen.
  • Active to Passive: Nobody has seen the ghost. / The ghost has not been seen.
  • Passive to Active (missing agent): The glass has been broken. / Somebody has broken the glass.

6. Two Objects (Direct and Indirect)

Active Structure: Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object Passive Structure 1: Indirect Object + be + V3 + Direct Object + by + Subject Passive Structure 2: Direct Object + be + V3 + to/for + Indirect Object + by + Subject
  • Active to Passive: The teacher gave the students extra marks. / The students were given extra marks by the teacher.
  • Active to Passive: I sent her a bouquet of flowers. / She was sent a bouquet of flowers by me.
  • Passive to Active: A well-paying job was offered to him by them. / They offered him a well-paying job.

02 Context Clues and Sentence Logic

Determining the direction requires carefully reading the instruction in the brackets. If the instruction states Change into passive voice you must locate the object and move it to the subject position. If the instruction states Change into active voice you must locate the by phrase (or implied agent) and move it to the subject position.

Determining the Agent in Passive to Active

If the passive sentence lacks a by phrase you must add an indefinite subject such as somebody people or they during the conversion to active voice.

  • Passive: My wallet has been stolen. / Active: Somebody has stolen my wallet.
  • Passive: English is spoken all over the world. / Active: People speak English all over the world.

03 SEE Appeared Questions

Question 1 (Active to Passive)
Everybody should follow the rules sincerely.
Answer: Rules should be followed sincerely by everybody.
Explanation: Modal should takes be and V3. The object rules becomes the subject.
Question 2 (Active to Passive)
We must not use the computer in full brightness.
Answer: The computer must not be used in full brightness by us.
Explanation: Negative modal structure. Object computer becomes subject.
Question 3 (Active to Passive)
Somebody knocked on the door.
Answer: The door was knocked on.
Explanation: The indefinite pronoun somebody is dropped. The preposition on remains attached to the verb.
Question 4 (Passive to Active)
The culprit was sent to prison by the police.
Answer: The police sent the culprit to prison.
Explanation: Changing passive to active. The agent police becomes the subject.
Question 5 (Passive to Active)
Her wallet has been stolen.
Answer: Somebody has stolen her wallet.
Explanation: Changing passive to active. An indefinite subject somebody is added.
Question 6 (Passive to Active)
The chapter will be read next time.
Answer: Somebody will read the chapter next time.
Explanation: Changing passive to active. An indefinite subject is added.
Question 7 (Active to Passive)
Kumari threw the garbage into the container.
Answer: The garbage was thrown into the container by Kumari.
Explanation: Past Simple tense. Object garbage becomes subject.
Question 8 (Active to Passive)
Open the door.
Answer: Let the door be opened.
Explanation: Imperative sentence. Uses the Let + object + be + V3 structure.
Question 9 (Active to Passive)
People speak English all over the world.
Answer: English is spoken all over the world.
Explanation: The agent people is general and dropped in the passive form.
Question 10 (Active to Passive)
Who broke the window?
Answer: By whom was the window broken?
Explanation: Interrogative sentence. Who changes to By whom.

04 Common Mistakes to Avoid

A letter is wrote by him. A letter is written by him. Use the past participle V3 not the past simple V2.
A letter is written from him. A letter is written by him. Use by to indicate the agent.
The work has done. The work has been done. Present perfect passive requires been.
The work will done. The work will be done. Future passive requires be.
Close the door passively. Let the door be closed. Imperatives require the Let structure.
He was made work. He was made to work. Active make takes V1 but passive make takes to V1.
Her wallet has been stolen by somebody. Somebody has stolen her wallet. When converting to active do not use by somebody use the indefinite subject directly.
