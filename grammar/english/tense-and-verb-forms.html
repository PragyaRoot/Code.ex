01 The Twelve Tenses

1 Present Simple

Structure: Subject + V1 (or V-s/es)

Usage: Universal truths, habitual actions and scheduled events.

  • Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
  • She goes to school every day.
2 Present Continuous

Structure: Subject + am/is/are + V-ing

Usage: Actions happening right now or temporary situations.

  • He is studying for his exams right now.
  • They are staying at a hotel this week.
3 Present Perfect

Structure: Subject + has/have + V3

Usage: Past actions with a connection to the present, unstated time or recently completed actions.

  • I have finished my homework.
  • She has just arrived at the station.
4 Present Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + has/have been + V-ing

Usage: Actions that started in the past and are still continuing or just stopped.

  • He has been working here since 2010.
  • They have been playing football for two hours.
5 Past Simple

Structure: Subject + V2

Usage: Completed actions in the past with a specific time.

  • I visited Pokhara last year.
  • She wrote a letter yesterday.
6 Past Continuous

Structure: Subject + was/were + V-ing

Usage: Ongoing actions in the past often interrupted by another action.

  • I was reading a book when the phone rang.
  • They were playing outside at 5 PM.
7 Past Perfect

Structure: Subject + had + V3

Usage: An action completed before another action in the past.

  • The train had left before we reached the station.
  • She had finished cooking before the guests arrived.
8 Past Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + had been + V-ing

Usage: An ongoing action in the past that happened before another past action.

  • He had been waiting for two hours before the bus finally came.
  • They had been living in London before they moved to Nepal.
9 Future Simple

Structure: Subject + will + V1

Usage: Predictions, spontaneous decisions and future facts.

  • I will help you with your project.
  • It will rain tomorrow.
10 Future Continuous

Structure: Subject + will be + V-ing

Usage: Actions that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

  • I will be taking my exam this time tomorrow.
  • They will be traveling to Kathmandu next week.
11 Future Perfect

Structure: Subject + will have + V3

Usage: Actions that will be completed before a specific time in the future.

  • By next month I will have submitted my project.
  • She will have finished her degree by 2026.
12 Future Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + will have been + V-ing

Usage: Actions that will have been ongoing for a duration up to a point in the future.

  • By next year he will have been working here for a decade.
  • In June they will have been living in this house for five years.

02 Context Clues and Sentence Logic

Determining the correct tense requires identifying explicit keywords and understanding the sequence of events.

Keyword Identification

Keyword in SentenceTense Required
always, usually, every dayPresent Simple
now, at the moment, currentlyPresent Continuous
already, yet, just, recentlyPresent Perfect
since, for (with present context)Present Perfect Continuous
yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010Past Simple
while, when (past context)Past Continuous
before, after, by the timePast Perfect
tomorrow, next week, soonFuture Simple
this time tomorrowFuture Continuous
by + future timeFuture Perfect

Logical Sequencing

When keywords are absent, examine the relationship between actions.

  • Two past actions: The action that happened first requires Past Perfect. The action that happened second requires Past Simple.
  • Duration leading to present: An action starting in the past and continuing to the present requires Present Perfect Continuous.
  • Future deadlines: An action that will finish before a specific future time requires Future Perfect.
  • Simultaneous past actions: Two actions happening at the same time in the past require Past Continuous.

03 SEE Appeared Questions

Question 1
Raima ......... (finish) all her homework before her teacher asked her to do it.
Answer: had finished
Context Clue: The word "before" indicates a past action that happened before another past action. Past Perfect is required.
Question 2
Before the police came, the thief ......... (run) away.
Answer: had run
Context Clue: "Before" indicates the running happened first. Past Perfect is required.
Question 3
By next week, I ......... (submit) my project work.
Answer: will have submitted
Context Clue: "By" plus future time requires Future Perfect.
Question 4
When I ......... (open) the fridge, I saw a packet of sausages.
Answer: opened
Context Clue: "When" refers to a specific completed action in the past. Past Simple is required.
Question 5
The police ......... (catch) the kidnappers recently.
Answer: have caught
Context Clue: "Recently" requires Present Perfect.
Question 6
My father ......... (work) in the kitchen garden at the moment.
Answer: is working
Context Clue: "At the moment" requires Present Continuous.
Question 7
While Sanskriti was preparing food, she ......... (watch) TV.
Answer: was watching
Context Clue: "While" indicates two simultaneous past actions. Past Continuous is required.
Question 8
By the time I reached home, my father ......... (leave) already.
Answer: had left
Context Clue: "By the time" indicates an action completed before another past action. Past Perfect is required.
Question 9
She ......... (pass) her SEE by 2082 BS.
Answer: will have passed
Context Clue: "By" plus future time requires Future Perfect.
Question 10
As soon as the teacher ......... (enter) the class, all the students stood up.
Answer: entered
Context Clue: "As soon as" indicates an immediate past action. Past Simple is required.

04 Common Mistakes to Avoid

I am knowing him. I know him. Stative verbs are not used in continuous form.
She is having a car. She has a car. Have for possession is not used in continuous form.
If it will rain, we will stay. If it rains, we will stay. Never use will in the if-clause.
He has went. He has gone. Use V3, not V2, after has/have/had.
I have saw it. I have seen it. Use V3, not V2, after has/have/had.
Before I came, he finishes. Before I came, he had finished. Before requires Past Perfect.
While I read, he came. While I was reading, he came. While requires Past Continuous.
