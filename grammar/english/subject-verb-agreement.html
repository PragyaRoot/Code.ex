Rules governing the correspondence between subjects and verbs in number and person. Includes singular and plural forms, collective nouns, indefinite pronouns and exceptional cases. This page includes complete rules, SEE appeared questions with complete solutions, common mistakes and practice exercises.

01 Core Rules

Rule 1 Basic Agreement

A singular subject takes a singular verb. A plural subject takes a plural verb.

  • He writes a letter.
  • They write a letter.
Rule 2 Compound Subjects

Two subjects joined by and take a plural verb.

  • Ram and Shyam are friends.
Exception: If the two nouns refer to the same person or thing the verb is singular.
Example: Bread and butter is my breakfast.
Rule 3 Proximity Rule

When subjects are joined by or, nor, either...or, neither...nor the verb agrees with the subject nearest to it.

  • Either Ram or his friends are coming.
  • Neither the teacher nor the students were present.
Rule 4 Prepositional Phrases

Phrases beginning with with, along with, as well as, besides, including or in addition to do not change the number of the subject. The verb agrees with the first subject.

  • The teacher along with the students is present.
  • Ram as well as his friends has arrived.
Rule 5 Indefinite Pronouns

The following pronouns are always singular and take singular verbs: everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything, no one, nobody, nothing, each, either, neither, one, many a.

  • Everyone has finished the work.
  • Each of the students was given a prize.
  • Many a man has tried and failed.
Rule 6 Plural Indefinite Pronouns

The words both, few, many and several are always plural and take plural verbs.

  • Both are guilty.
  • Many have applied for the job.
Rule 7 Collective Nouns

Collective nouns such as team, family, crowd, committee and government take a singular verb when acting as a single unit and a plural verb when acting as individuals.

  • The team has won the match.
  • The team are arguing among themselves.
Rule 8 Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns such as water, milk, furniture, information, advice and luggage are always singular and take singular verbs.

  • Water is essential for life.
  • The furniture is new.
Rule 9 Nouns with Plural Form but Singular Meaning

Nouns ending in s such as news, mathematics, physics, economics, politics, ethics and measles are singular and take singular verbs.

  • The news is good.
  • Mathematics is my favourite subject.
Rule 10 Nouns Always Plural

Nouns such as scissors, trousers, spectacles, shorts, jeans, pliers, clothes, cattle, police and people are always plural and take plural verbs.

  • The scissors are sharp.
  • The police have arrested the thief.
Note: A pair of scissors is considered singular.
Rule 11 A Number of vs The Number of

A number of takes a plural verb. The number of takes a singular verb.

  • A number of students are absent.
  • The number of students is increasing.
Rule 12 Distance Time Money and Weight

When a distance time sum of money or weight is considered as a single unit the verb is singular.

  • Ten kilometres is a long distance.
  • Fifty rupees is not enough.
Rule 13 Fractions and Percentages

The verb agrees with the noun following the fraction or percentage.

  • Half of the cake is eaten.
  • Half of the students are absent.
Rule 14 One of

One of is followed by a plural noun but takes a singular verb.

  • One of my friends is a doctor.
Rule 15 Relative Pronouns

The verb in a relative clause agrees with the antecedent.

  • I who am your friend will help you.
  • He is the boy who has won the prize.
Rule 16 Gerunds and Infinitives

When a gerund or an infinitive acts as the subject the verb is singular.

  • Swimming is a good exercise.
  • To err is human.

02 Context Clues and Sentence Logic

Identifying the true subject is the most critical step. Prepositional phrases and descriptive clauses often separate the subject from the verb.

Isolating the Subject

Cross out any phrase beginning with a preposition such as of, in, at, by or with. The remaining noun is the true subject.

  • The quality (of the mangoes) is good.
  • The box (of chocolates) was delivered.

Determining Singular or Plural

Check if the subject is a single entity, a collective group or a plural noun. Look out for indefinite pronouns which often dictate a singular verb despite referring to multiple people.

  • Everyone (singular) is here.
  • The police (plural) are investigating.

03 SEE Appeared Questions

Question 1
The healthy people (is/ are/ was/ has) recognized by their work stamina.
Answer: are
Explanation: People is a plural noun requiring a plural verb.
Question 2
Many sports academies (work/ are working/ worked/ have been working) hard on health aspects for a long time.
Answer: have been working
Explanation: Academies is plural. The phrase for a long time indicates an action continuing from the past to the present requiring Present Perfect Continuous.
Question 3
The number of hours he studies (is/ are/ was/ were) sufficient.
Answer: is
Explanation: The phrase "The number of" takes a singular verb.
Question 4
Mathematics and Science (is/ are/ was/ were) his favourite subjects.
Answer: are
Explanation: Two subjects joined by "and" require a plural verb.
Question 5
Each of his teachers (love/ loves/ loved/ loving) him.
Answer: loves
Explanation: Each is a singular indefinite pronoun requiring a singular verb.
Question 6
Neither he nor his friends (waste/ wastes/ wasting/ wasted) time.
Answer: waste
Explanation: The proximity rule applies. Friends is the nearest subject and it is plural.
Question 7
One of his hobbies (is/ are/ was/ were) reading.
Answer: is
Explanation: "One of" takes a singular verb.
Question 8
The furniture in the room (are/ is/ were/ was) new.
Answer: is
Explanation: Furniture is an uncountable noun requiring a singular verb.
Question 9
A number of books (is/ are/ was/ were) missing from the library.
Answer: are
Explanation: "A number of" takes a plural verb.
Question 10
Neither of the students (speaks/ speak/ spoke/ speaking) English.
Answer: speaks
Explanation: Neither is a singular indefinite pronoun. Note that in question tags it becomes they but in the main clause it takes a singular verb.

04 Common Mistakes to Avoid

The news are good. The news is good. News is singular.
Mathematics are difficult. Mathematics is difficult. Mathematics is singular.
My trousers is new. My trousers are new. Trousers is always plural.
The police has arrested him. The police have arrested him. Police is always plural.
Each of the boys were present. Each of the boys was present. Each is singular.
One of my friends are a doctor. One of my friends is a doctor. One of takes a singular verb.
A number of students is absent. A number of students are absent. A number of takes a plural verb.
The number of students are increasing. The number of students is increasing. The number of takes a singular verb.
Neither Ram nor Shyam are coming. Neither Ram nor Shyam is coming. Proximity rule. Shyam is singular.
Ram as well as his friends have come. Ram as well as his friends has come. Verb agrees with the first subject Ram.
Everyone have finished. Everyone has finished. Everyone is singular.
Ten kilometres are a long distance. Ten kilometres is a long distance. Distance is treated as a single unit.
Fifty percent of the work are done. Fifty percent of the work is done. Work is uncountable.
