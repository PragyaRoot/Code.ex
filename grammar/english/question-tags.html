01 Definition

A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to confirm, check, or seek agreement.

Examples

  • You are coming, aren't you?
  • She doesn't like tea, does she?

The Golden Rule

Positive Statement - Negative Tag Negative Statement - Positive Tag
Statement TypeTag TypeExample
PositiveNegativeShe is here, isn't she?
NegativePositiveShe isn't here, is she?

03 Rules of Question Tags

Rule 1 Auxiliary Verbs (is, am, are, was, were)
StatementTag
She is a doctor,isn't she?
They are playing,aren't they?
I am late,aren't I?
He was present,wasn't he?
We were wrong,weren't we?
It is cold,isn't it?
You are tired,aren't you?
Special Case: "I am" becomes "aren't I?" (never "amn't I?")
Rule 2 Present Simple (do/does)
StatementTag
You like tea,don't you?
You don't like tea,do you?
She plays tennis,doesn't she?
She doesn't play tennis,does she?
He works hard,doesn't he?
They live here,don't they?
We need help,don't we?
It rains a lot,doesn't it?
Rule: Positive statements without an auxiliary verb take don't or doesn't. Negative statements take do or does.
Rule 3 Past Simple (did)
StatementTag
You went there,didn't you?
You didn't go there,did you?
She cooked food,didn't she?
She didn't cook food,did she?
They played well,didn't they?
He came late,didn't he?
I saw him,didn't I?
Rule: Positive statements take didn't. Negative statements take did.
Rule 4 Present Perfect (has/have)
StatementTag
You have finished,haven't you?
She has gone,hasn't she?
They have arrived,haven't they?
He has eaten,hasn't he?
Rule 5 Past Perfect (had)
StatementTag
You had left,hadn't you?
She had eaten,hadn't she?
They had gone,hadn't they?
Rule 6 Modals
StatementTag
You can swim,can't you?
She could help,couldn't she?
He will come,won't he?
They would agree,wouldn't they?
We shall go,shan't we?
You should try,shouldn't you?
It might rain,mightn't it?
We must obey,mustn't we?
Rule 7 Imperatives
StatementTag
Close the door,will you?
Don't go out,will you?
Please help me,will you?
Have a seat,won't you?
Rules:
• Commands take will you?
• Invitations take won't you? (polite)
• Let's takes shall we?
• Let me takes may I?
• Let him, her, them takes will you?

Rule 9 'Have' - Possession vs Action
StatementTagReason
You have a car,don't you?Possession takes don't
You have finished,haven't you?Action (perfect) takes haven't
She has a dog,doesn't she?Possession takes doesn't
She has gone,hasn't she?Action (perfect) takes hasn't
He has a headache,doesn't he?Possession takes doesn't
He has left,hasn't he?Action (perfect) takes hasn't
Rule: Usage varies by construction. If "have" is an auxiliary (perfect tense), use have, has, had. If "have" means possess, use do, does, did, though haven't is also common in British English.

Rule 12 Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns like neither, either, someone, nobody, everyone can take the plural pronoun they or the singular pronoun it depending on the noun they refer to. For people, they is common. For things, it is common.

StatementTag
Neither of them came,did they?
Either of the boys can go,can't they?
Everyone is here,aren't they?
Someone called,didn't they?
Nobody came,did they?
None of them helped,did they?
Exception: For things (not people), use it:
• Nothing happened, did it?
• Everything is fine, isn't it?

StatementTag

Rule 17 'You'd Better' takes 'Hadn't You?'
StatementTag

Rule 18 'You'd Like' takes 'Wouldn't You?'

StatementTag

Rule 20 'Needn't' takes 'Need'
StatementTag

She needn't come,need she?
They needn't wait,need they?

09 Decision Flowchart

Is it Let's? ↓ YES - shall we? ↓ NO - Is it an imperative? ↓ YES - will you? (or won't you? for invitation) ↓ NO - Is the subject I? ↓ YES - aren't I? ↓ NO - Is it You'd better? ↓ YES - hadn't you? ↓ NO - Does it contain a negative word (never, hardly, nobody, no one, nothing)? ↓ YES - Use positive tag ↓ NO - Is there an auxiliary verb or modal? ↓ YES - Use that auxiliary in opposite form ↓ NO - Use do/does/did in opposite form

11 Frequently Asked Questions

What is a question tag in English grammar?

A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to confirm, check, or seek agreement. For example, You are coming, aren't you?

What is the golden rule for question tags?

The golden rule is that a positive statement takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive tag.

What is the question tag for I am?

The question tag for I am is aren't I? For example, I am late, aren't I?
